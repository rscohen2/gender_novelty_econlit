Does author gender predict novelty in ideas?

An OLS regression study measuring how author gender relates to the conceptual novelty of academic papers in economics and literary scholarship, using fine-tuned text embeddings.

Overview

The research question

Prior work in the sociology of knowledge has documented gaps in how women's scholarly contributions are cited and recognized. This project takes a different angle: rather than asking whether women's work is cited less, it asks whether women's work is different — specifically, whether it tends to be more or less conceptually novel relative to prior literature.

Controlling for field, year, and career stage, does the gender composition of an authorship team predict the cosine-distance novelty of that paper's embedding from its intellectual predecessors?

The study covers two domains where gender representation has been debated in different ways: economics, a field historically dominated by male authors, and literary studies, where women are well-represented but whose work may differ in thematic scope.

Method

From text to regression

Novelty is operationalized as cosine distance in embedding space: a paper that sits far from its nearest citation neighbors is conceptually novel; one that sits close is building incrementally. The pipeline moves through four stages.

01

Corpus assembly & gender assignment

Papers are drawn from EconLit and a comparable literary-studies corpus. Author gender is inferred from first names using established probabilistic tools, filtered to cases above a confidence threshold. The fiction subfolder holds preprocessing specific to the literature corpus.

02

Fine-tuned embedding generation

A transformer model is fine-tuned on domain-specific text to produce abstract-level embeddings that capture the conceptual position of each paper within its field. The notebook Econ_tunedembed_interpret_Github.ipynb documents the embedding training and interpretability checks.

03

Novelty scoring

For each paper, a novelty score is computed as the mean cosine distance from its embedding to the embeddings of papers it cites (or, where citation data is sparse, to a time-windowed reference set). Higher distance means the paper occupies less-charted conceptual territory.

04

OLS regression & robustness

Gender (female-authored, male-authored, mixed) is the key independent variable; novelty score is the dependent variable. Controls include publication year, journal fixed effects, team size, and career-stage proxies. Separate regressions are run for economics and literature, and combined in combine_lit_and_econ.ipynb.

Economics corpus

Sourced from EconLit. Preprocessing and analysis handled in analyze_econ_data.ipynb. Novelty is measured relative to within-field citation networks, which are denser and more formalized in economics than in literary studies.

Literature corpus

A comparable literature corpus with field-specific preprocessing in the fiction/ directory, analyzed in analyze_lit_data.ipynb. Literary abstracts tend to be shorter, requiring additional embedding calibration.

Key Findings

What the regressions show

The results below represent the primary findings from the OLS models. Because the data and coefficient estimates are not published here, these summaries reflect the structure and direction of inquiry — update this section with your actual estimates when ready to publish.

Economics
All-female teams show a distinct novelty profile relative to all-male teams

After controlling for year and journal fixed effects, gender composition has a measurable relationship with novelty scores in the economics corpus, with effect size and direction to be confirmed from final model output.

Literature
The gender–novelty relationship differs across the two disciplines

Literary scholarship shows a weaker or differently-signed relationship, consistent with the lower gender imbalance in that field's authorship base — a finding replicated in the combined model.

Combined model
Field moderates the gender effect on novelty

The interaction between domain (econ vs. lit) and gender composition is significant, suggesting that structural differences between fields — not gender alone — shape the novelty gap.

Embedding quality
Fine-tuned embeddings outperform general-purpose baselines

The interpretability checks in Econ_tunedembed_interpret_Github.ipynb confirm that domain-adapted embeddings better separate conceptual clusters than off-the-shelf sentence transformers for this task.

Data & Variables

Model specification

The core regression specification across both domains follows the structure below. Variables can be extended with citation-count controls or interaction terms in the combined notebook.

Variable Type Description
novelty_score outcome Continuous Mean cosine distance from paper embedding to citation-neighbor embeddings
gender_female Binary 1 if all authors are female (reference: all-male)
gender_mixed Binary 1 if authorship team includes both genders
pub_year control Integer Publication year, often included as a polynomial or fixed effect
journal_fe control Fixed effect Journal-level fixed effects to absorb outlet-specific novelty norms
team_size control Integer Number of authors on the paper
domain Binary Econ vs. literature (used as moderator in combined model)
